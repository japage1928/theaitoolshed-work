Before You Publish Your First Faceless Product Video: A Plain Checklist

You made a short product video. No face on camera, script drafted with AI help, visuals generated, music underneath. It feels done.

It might not be. Three unglamorous checks decide whether that video is useful or just noise. Get them wrong and you risk a flagged account, dead links, or comments saying the video lied about the product. Get them right and the video does its job quietly.

Here is the checklist I wish someone had handed me first.

1. The script check: can you defend every sentence?

Faceless videos make it easy to oversell. The script sounds confident, the voiceover sounds sure, and suddenly a $25 gadget has "amazing revolutionary features." Stop there. Read the script out loud and ask: is this literally true of the thing in the box?

Honest visuals matter just as much. If the video shows the product doing something you have not verified, cut that shot. One checked script beats three hyped ones. People can smell a claim that came from a stock photo.

2. Disclosure: say the plain thing, up front

If you earn a commission when someone buys through your link, say so. Not buried at the bottom of the description. Say it in the video and in the description, in plain words, something like: "I earn from qualifying purchases as an Amazon Associate."

This is not just politeness. The FTC requires clear disclosure of the financial relationship, and Amazon has its own rules about how associates present links. Disclosure does not hurt trust. Hiding the relationship and getting caught does.

3. The link path: click your own link before anyone else does

This is the check everyone skips and the one that costs the most. Open the video description, click your affiliate link, and follow it like a stranger would:

  • Does it land on the exact product, or a search page, or a listing that changed?
  • Does it work on your phone, not just your laptop?
  • If the price or availability changed since you filmed, does anything in your script now contradict the page?

A working affiliate link path sounds obvious. It breaks more often than you think. Listings get merged, sellers change, variants go out of stock. Five minutes of clicking saves you from a video that sends people nowhere.

4. Know the Associate rules that bite beginners

A few Amazon Associates rules catch new people off guard:

  • Do not state prices in the video. Prices change constantly. A "only $24.99" line turns into a lie by Tuesday.
  • Do not make claims about ratings you cannot verify right now.
  • Keep your account in good standing by reading the operating agreement, not skimming a summary of it.

The setup and application side is its own job. Walk through it once, carefully, before you publish anything.

5. The boring production pass

Short is fine. Thirty to ninety seconds covers most products. Check the audio on phone speakers, add captions (most people watch muted), and cut anything that does not help someone decide. If a shot does not answer "what is it, what does it do, is it what I need," it is filler.

What ties this together
