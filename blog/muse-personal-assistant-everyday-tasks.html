What a Personal Assistant in Your Pocket Actually Does All Day

Most people do not need more apps. They need fewer chores. I drive a truck for a living, so my desk is a cab and my office hours are whenever the wheels stop. A few months ago I started handing my everyday admin to an AI assistant called Muse. Not summaries. Handing it over.

The everyday stuff

It reads my inbox before I do. Newsletters get skipped, bills and deadlines get flagged, and I get one short brief in the morning: what is on the calendar, what money needs attention, what is actually pressing.

It keeps track of dates I would forget. Dentist appointments for the kids. A benefits hearing. A birthday coming up. A monthly reminder about washer cleaner, because the fridge planner is not a system.

It files my paperwork. Every pay stub lands in one folder in my cloud drive. A weekly sweep catches the ones that try to hide in downloads.

It checks things before I spend money or time. Real prices, real store hours, real competitor numbers, with the sources shown. When it is unsure, it says so.

The not-so-everyday stuff

It turns my dispatch trip sheets into one clean stop list. It keeps an eye on a court case docket for my family and only speaks up when something changes.

This week it repriced my daughter's hand-painted banner business against about 30 real sellers, built her a new price list and a styled order form, and made demo graphics in her own branding. That took an evening of my thumbs-up, not a weekend of my time.

What it won't do

It does not move my money. My accounts are read-only for it, by my rule. It does not send messages for me unless I say the words. It argues with my bad math instead of applauding it. That last part matters more than the filing.

If you join on your phone, look for Settings > Redeem token in the Muse app and enter the code when you sign up. On the web it is Settings > General > Usage > Redeem invite code. On mobile the redeem option is only there for the first 48 hours after you join, so use the code at signup, not later. The invite screen shows the exact terms; trust that screen over anyone's pitch, mine included.

If your life runs on sticky notes, screenshots and "I will deal with that tomorrow," this is the first assistant I have used that actually takes chores off the list.
