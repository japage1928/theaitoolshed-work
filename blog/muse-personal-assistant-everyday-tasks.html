What a Personal Assistant in Your Pocket Actually Does All Day

I'm an over-the-road truck driver. My desk is a cab. My filing cabinet used to be a downloads folder with four hundred files in it, and my to-do list was a planner on the fridge at home, three states away. A few months ago I handed the admin to an AI assistant called Muse. Not summaries of my chores. The chores.

5:30 in the morning, parked wherever last night ended

I come off a 10-hour reset and there's a short brief waiting on my phone. What's on the calendar today. Which bills need attention this week. Anything from dispatch that actually needs me. The newsletters, the noise, the "just checking in" emails got skipped before I ever saw them.

I read it with coffee. Ten minutes later I'm rolling, and my head is on the road instead of on forty unread emails.

The list it keeps so I don't have to

It tracks the dates I'd forget. Dentist appointments for the kids. A benefits hearing. A birthday next week. Even a reminder on the 18th of every month to run cleaner through the washing machine, because the fridge planner is a suggestion, not a system.

It files my paperwork. Every pay stub from the company lands in one folder in my cloud drive. 101 of them at last count. A sweep runs every Monday and catches the ones that tried to hide in downloads. I haven't dug through a downloads folder looking for a pay stub in months.

It checks things before I spend money or time. Real prices, real store hours, real competitor numbers, with the sources shown so I can verify. And when it's unsure, it says so. That alone puts it ahead of most software.

The stuff I didn't buy it for

Dispatch sends a trip sheet, and I get back one clean stop list: addresses, appointment times, who to call. No squinting at a PDF at a fuel stop.

It watches a court docket for my family and only speaks up when something changes. Silence from it means nothing changed. That's the whole feature, and it's a good one.

This week it repriced my daughter's hand-painted banner business against about 30 real sellers, rebuilt her price list, and made her an order form in her own branding. She was charging $50 for four hours of painting. She isn't anymore. The whole thing took me an evening of thumbs-ups, not a weekend.

The part that actually sold me

It argues with me.

I had an idea for a per diem tracker app for truck drivers. Decent idea, I thought. It went and checked the tax law and told me company drivers can't deduct per diem anymore. Hasn't been allowed for years. The product was dead before I built it, and it saved me a month of work on something nobody could legally use.

Most apps that want your money will tell you your idea is great. This one showed me the math and killed it. That's when I started trusting it with the real stuff.

What it won't do

It does not move my money. My accounts are read-only for it, by my rule. It does not send messages or emails for me unless I say the words. It does not applaud bad math to keep me happy.

That last part matters more than the filing.
