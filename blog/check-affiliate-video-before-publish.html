How to Check an Affiliate Video Before You Publish It

The edit is done. Do not publish it yet. Run five plain checks first, in order, and fix what you find while it is still private.

Most affiliate video problems are not editing problems. The video looks fine. Then a viewer clicks and lands on the wrong size, or reads a claim the product page does not support, or never sees the disclosure at all. Those are cheaper to fix before you post than after.

This pass takes about ten minutes once you have done it twice. If you have not made your first one yet, start there first: How to Make Your First Faceless Product Video Without Showing Your Face covers picking the product and writing the script.

1. Read the script against the product page

Open the product page on one side and your script on the other. Go line by line. Every fact in the script needs a match on the page or in notes from your own use. Size, weight, what is in the box, what it runs on, and any limit you mention.

Pay close attention to small words that change the meaning. Words like always, never, and best invite trouble if the facts do not back them up. If a line says the product holds a full day of charge and the page says up to a set number of hours under certain use, change the line to match the page. Plain and correct beats strong and wrong.

If you used ChatGPT to help draft the script, this step matters even more. The tool will write a smooth line whether the fact is right or not. You bring the facts. You do the checking. That habit is covered in How to Use Muse: A Beginner's Guide to Your First AI Assistant.

2. Watch the finished video twice

Watch it once with the sound on, the way a viewer will. Then watch it again with the sound off and the captions on, if your platform shows them. A lot of people watch on a phone in a public place with no sound. If the video only makes sense with audio, add clear text on screen for the main points.

While you watch, look at the visuals. Does the clip show the right product, color, and size. Does it show a feature the real product does not have. Generated visuals can add a button or a second piece that does not exist. If you spot that, replace the clip.

3. Check the disclosure where it actually sits

Disclosure only works if a person sees it before they act on the link. Check three places. Is it said or shown early in the video, not only at the end. Is it in the description, in plain words a new viewer can understand. Is it still readable on a phone screen, without tapping to expand anything.

Use simple wording. For example: This video uses affiliate links. If you buy through my link, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Then check the current rules for the platform and for Amazon Associates before you post, because those rules can change. If your wording and the rules do not match, follow the rules.

4. Click the link like a buyer

Do this on your phone, not only on your computer. Tap the exact link a viewer will tap. Wait for the page to load fully. Read the product name at the top. Check the variant, the color or size your video talked about. Check the price shown, and do not quote a price in the video unless you also say prices change.

If anything is off, fix the link and test again. A video that sends people to the wrong version teaches viewers not to trust the next one.

5. Read the title and description last

Read the title out loud. Does it promise only what the video actually shows. If the video covers one basic use, the title should not promise a full review or a test you did not do. Cut any word you cannot support with what is on screen.

Then read the description. It should say, in two or three short lines, what the product is, who it fits, and one limit worth knowing. Put the link after that, with the disclosure close to it. If someone reads only the description, they should still get an honest picture.

A short list to keep by your desk

  • Script facts match the product page, line by line.
  • Visuals show the right product, size, and features.
  • Disclosure sits early in the video and in the description.
  • Link opens the exact product and version, tested on a phone.
  • Title and description promise only what the video shows.

Run the same list every time, in the same order. Write down any fix you make more than once. That fix belongs earlier in your process, when you write the script, not at the end when you are tired and ready to post.
